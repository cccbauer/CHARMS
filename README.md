# CHARMS Project Code & Materials

Clinical High At-Risk Mental State (CHARMS)

This repository contains research protocols, analysis pipelines, and implementation materials for a novel mindfulness-based real-time fMRI neurofeedback (mbNF) intervention targeting repetitive negative thinking (RNT) and Default Mode Network (DMN) hyperconnectivity in adolescents at risk for serious mental illness.

 Background & Rationale

Increasing numbers of adolescents are presenting for mental health care with symptoms consistent with serious mental illnesses (SMI) such as schizophrenia and depression. Many SMI exhibit pluripotent risk profiles during the prodromal phase, underscoring the need to develop novel interventions that target transdiagnostic mechanisms and ameliorate distressing symptoms in adolescents at risk for SMI.

Key Research Insights

Repetitive Negative Thinking (RNT): Maladaptive, repetitive, intrusive, unproductive thought patterns associated with broad range of SMI, symptom severity, treatment non-response and relapse
Neural Correlates: RNT characterized by elevated functional connectivity within the default mode network (DMN)
DMN Hyperconnectivity: Consistently demonstrated in SMI across multiple studies
Mindfulness Limitations: While mindfulness meditation reduces RNT, it may be insufficient alone due to symptom interference with skill acquisition
